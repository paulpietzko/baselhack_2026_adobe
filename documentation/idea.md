# The Explorable Web

> Working name. The web is the world. Your intent is the lens. The interface is the view.

Our response to Adobe's *The Post-Website Business* challenge at BaselHack 2026.

## The vision

In five to ten years, AI agents do most of the reading on the web. So what do people do? We think the web stops feeling like a collection of websites and starts feeling like a connected world of places, people, products and capabilities, which you explore by following your curiosity.

Most answers to this challenge ask how a business talks to an agent. We build on that and ask the next question: **if agents can reach everything, what does exploring feel like for people?**

Three principles:

- **Semantic zoom:** move from a city to a venue to one detail of that venue.
- **An interface shaped by intent:** map, 3D place, tile or filter, depending on what you're curious about right now.
- **Continuous context:** jump from a club to a costume shop without starting over. The occasion, your choices and your constraints come with you.

People stay in charge. AI handles relevance and retrieval, and people choose what to look at and confirm anything with consequences.

## What a local business publishes

A club owner shouldn't need to write an API or configure MCP. They point at what they already have, correct what's wrong and press Publish. The service hosts the agent-readable presence for them.

We separate two things:

- **Business truth:** facts, prices, policies and availability, which the owner approves.
- **Business intent:** what the owner hopes to achieve. It can shape how they're presented, but never truth or ranking.

## The demo

A fictional Basel club, Hafenlicht, publishes its Halloween party. Later, a group of friends with no plan asks what to do in Basel. The city unfolds, night falls when they ask for a party, and the club's published record brings it onto the map. They step inside, explore music, floors, dress code and tickets in any order, jump from the dress code to costumes, and get sidetracked by a casino when a costume costs CHF 180.
